1. SYSTEM OVERVIEW & ITEM BOUNDARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Model scope: This synthetic item describes the primary electronic braking path.
Independent emergency braking paths are outside its scope and are not assumed.
Sensor-agreement tolerance and communication timeouts are configurable; their
numeric calibration is outside this item definition.
The Electronic Brake-by-Wire (BBW) & Regenerative Braking Subsystem (AUTO-BBW-01) 
is a function-level architecture responsible for capturing driver deceleration 
intent at the brake pedal, blending regenerative electric motor braking with 
friction braking, and commanding the 4-corner electro-mechanical brake caliper 
actuators to press the brake pads against the wheel rotors.

System Boundary:
- Physical & Sensor Entry: Brake Pedal Sensor Unit (COMP-BPSU) in the cabin 
  pedal box zone, equipped with dual Hall-effect sensors and an exposed SWD 
  debug header (INT-SWD-HEADER).
- Diagnostic & Wireless Interfaces: Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE 5.0) workshop 
  service interface (INT-BLE-DIAG) and physical OBD-II CAN port (INT-OBD2-CAN) 
  terminated at the Vehicle Motion & Chassis Domain Controller (COMP-VDCM).
- Internal Control Networks:
  * Chassis & Pedal Sensor CAN-FD Bus (CONN-BRK-02) linking COMP-BPSU, 
    COMP-VDCM, and the Regenerative Inverter Controller (COMP-RIC).
  * Braking TSN Ethernet Link (CONN-BRK-03, 100BASE-T1) linking
    COMP-VDCM to the Electronic Brake Control Module (COMP-EBCM).
  * Wheel-End Brake Actuator CAN-FD Bus (CONN-BRK-04) routed from COMP-EBCM 
    into the 4 wheel wells to drive the Smart Electro-Mechanical Brake Caliper 
    Actuators (COMP-EMBA).


2. FUNCTIONAL SPECIFICATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1. FUNC-BRK-01: Driver Pedal Travel Sensing
-----------------------------------------------------
- Objective:
  Sample two redundant Hall-effect pedal travel sensors at 1 kHz and transmit
  driver pedal demand over CAN-FD to the chassis domain controller.

- Involved Components & Interfaces:
  COMP-BPSU -> [CONN-BRK-02: Chassis CAN-FD Bus (500 kbps / 2 Mbps)] -> COMP-VDCM.

- Functional Behavior & Data Flow:
  1. Sensor Sampling: The Brake Pedal Sensor Unit (COMP-BPSU, Infineon TLE4998S 
     + NXP S32K116 running Bare-Metal Firmware v1.0.4) samples two redundant
     Hall-effect travel sensors at 1 kHz via `pedal_feel_and_travel_encoder`
     (v1.0.4).
     Both readings must be within 0-100% travel, inclusive. Their absolute
     difference must be within the configured agreement tolerance. When both
     checks pass, BPSU uses their arithmetic mean as pedal demand and sets
     `demand_valid` to true. Otherwise, it sets `demand_valid` to false and does
     not select either sensor as correct. The travel value has no meaning when invalid.
  2. Signal Transmission: BPSU sends CAN-FD message `0x080`
     (`Pedal_Travel_Demand`, ASSET-BRK-01) over `CONN-BRK-02` to `COMP-VDCM`.
     The message contains `pedal_travel_percent`, `demand_valid`, and an E2E
     CRC-8 covering both fields. Invalid demand is distinct from a valid 0%
     reading, which represents a released pedal. BPSU transmits after each
     sampling cycle, including when demand is invalid.
  3. Pedal Demand Reception & Fallback: VDCM accepts a correctly formatted
     message with valid CRC-8, demand_valid true, and travel within 0-100%.
     The last accepted message replaces the stored demand and its local
     monotonic reception time. VDCM uses zero demand before the first accepted
     message. Invalid or missing messages leave the last accepted demand in
     use only until the configured timeout; they do not restart that interval.
     At timeout, VDCM marks driver demand unavailable and sets pedal-derived
     braking demand to zero. The next accepted message restores demand use.
     A valid 0% message releases pedal-derived demand immediately.
     This hold-then-zero response is a simplified primary-path model, not a
     production safety strategy. Loss of valid input can remove pedal-derived
     braking demand. Independent fallback braking is not modeled.

- Current Security & Operational Parameters (As-Built Baseline):
  * End-to-End (E2E) CRC-8 safety checksums are enabled (`e2e_crc_enabled: true`), 
    but cryptographic message authentication (AUTOSAR SecOC MAC) is disabled 
    (`secoc_mac_enabled: false`). VDCM verifies CRC-8 on pedal demand
    (`pedal_demand_e2e_crc_enabled: true`), but does not verify a MAC or
    freshness counter (`pedal_demand_mac_verification_enabled: false`,
    `pedal_demand_freshness_counter_verification_enabled: false`).
  * The SWD header (`INT-SWD-HEADER`) is unlocked
    (`jtag_swd_debug_port_locked: false`). Debugger access requires cabin access
    and physical access to the BPSU board in the pedal box. No additional debug
    authentication is required.
  * Pedal travel zero-point calibration storage is not write-protected
    (`calibration_eeprom_write_protected: false`) and can be modified through
    SWD debugger access. This is the modeled calibration write path; diagnostic
    or remote calibration writes are not established.

- Associated Cybersecurity Goals & Damage Scenarios:
  * Protected Asset: ASSET-BRK-01 (Driver Pedal Travel & Braking Demand Signal)
  * Security Properties: Integrity, Authenticity, Availability
  * Damage Scenario (DS-BRK-01): Insufficient braking during an emergency stop
    or unintended braking at highway speed can cause a collision. The collision
    can cause serious injury or death.
    Impact — Safety: Severe, Financial: Major, Operational: Severe, Privacy: Negligible.


2.2. FUNC-BRK-02: Brake Torque Blending & Dynamic Allocation
------------------------------------------------------------
- Objective:
  Calculate total vehicle deceleration demand, coordinate regenerative braking 
  torque with the traction inverter, and transmit friction braking torque 
  requests to the Electronic Brake Control Module.

- Involved Components & Interfaces:
  COMP-VDCM <-> [CONN-BRK-02: Chassis CAN-FD] <-> COMP-RIC
  COMP-VDCM -> [CONN-BRK-03: 100BASE-T1 TSN Ethernet] -> COMP-EBCM
  EXTERNAL_ATTACKER_SURFACE -> [CONN-BRK-01: BLE / OBD-II] -> COMP-VDCM.

- Functional Behavior & Data Flow:
  1. Torque Split Calculation: `COMP-VDCM` (Renesas R-Car S4 running Embedded 
     Linux 5.10.70 + AUTOSAR Adaptive) executes `brake_blending_coordinator` 
     (v2.3.1) to split deceleration demand between regenerative electric 
     braking and mechanical friction braking.
  2. Regenerative Feedback Loop: The Regenerative Inverter Controller (`COMP-RIC`, 
     TI C2000 running TI-RTOS `sys/bios` v6.83.00.18 with `regen_torque_inverter_app` 
     v4.1.2) applies negative motor torque and reports actual achieved 
     regenerative torque to `COMP-VDCM` via CAN ID `0x1A0` over `CONN-BRK-02`.
     RIC transmits this feedback periodically.
  3. Friction Demand Transmission: `COMP-VDCM` calculates friction torque demand
     as max(0, driver demand - credited regenerative contribution). Both inputs
     use the same torque basis, with positive values representing braking.
     It sends the friction torque request (`BrakeTorqueRequest`, SOME/IP Service
     `0x1020`, ASSET-BRK-02) over 100BASE-T1 TSN Ethernet (`CONN-BRK-03`,
     UDP) to the configured application port on `COMP-EBCM`. SOME/IP service
     discovery is outside the current model.
     - At startup, the credited regenerative contribution is zero.
     - Each correctly formatted feedback message that passes E2E CRC-8 updates
       the stored torque and local monotonic reception time.
     - Invalid messages are discarded without updating torque or reception time.
       The previous contribution remains in use until timeout.
     - When the configured reception timeout expires, VDCM credits zero
       regenerative contribution and requests the full available driver braking
       demand through friction braking. If pedal demand has also timed out,
       its contribution is zero under the pedal fallback rule.
     - The next accepted message restores blending with its reported torque.
     This fallback defines the requested torque, not the achieved deceleration.
  4. Workshop Diagnostics & IPC: `COMP-VDCM` uses `bluez` (v5.54) for BLE
     transport to the workshop diagnostic service. Normal BLE service access
     requires wireless proximity and pairing with the fixed six-digit passkey
     `000000`. The BLE diagnostic link is encrypted; OBD-II CAN is unencrypted.
     OBD-II access requires a physical connection to `INT-OBD2-CAN`.
     Both entry points submit diagnostic requests to VDCM. VDCM handles the
     supported requests; it does not forward arbitrary chassis CAN frames
     from either entry point to `CONN-BRK-02`.
     VDCM forwards EBCM-supported diagnostic requests from either entry point
     over DoIP on `CONN-BRK-03`, without additional client authorization.
     EBCM applies authorization separately for each requested operation.
     VDCM also runs `mosquitto` (v2.0.10, TCP port 1883) for internal
     SOME/IP-to-MQTT telemetry.

- Current Security & Operational Parameters (As-Built Baseline):
  * Software versions on COMP-VDCM: `linux_kernel` v5.10.70, `bluez`
    v5.54, and `mosquitto` v2.0.10.
    The synthetic kernel baseline is unmodified upstream Linux v5.10.70.
  * Software versions on COMP-RIC: TI `sys/bios` v6.83.00.18.
  * BLE workshop pairing uses a fixed six-digit passkey (`000000`). Link
    encryption does not add application authorization to diagnostic operations.
  * `brake_blending_coordinator` runs with root privileges (`runs_as_root: true`).
  * SOME/IP traffic over `CONN-BRK-03` is transmitted in plaintext without TLS, 
    IPsec, or MACsec (`someip_tls_or_ipsec_enabled: false`).
  * VDCM accepts UDS `0x2F` (InputOutputControlByIdentifier) brake-demand
    override requests through either diagnostic entry point without additional
    application authorization (`uds_0x2F_io_control_auth_required: false`).
    The override is permitted while driving
    (`allow_diagnostic_brake_override_while_driving: true`).
  * `COMP-VDCM` checks the E2E CRC-8 of regenerative torque feedback on CAN ID
    `0x1A0` (`regen_feedback_e2e_crc_enabled: true`). CRC detects transmission
    errors; it does not authenticate the sender.
  * `COMP-VDCM` does not verify a cryptographic MAC on this feedback
    (`regen_feedback_mac_verification_enabled: false`).
  * `COMP-VDCM` does not check a freshness counter on this feedback
    (`regen_feedback_freshness_counter_verification_enabled: false`).
  * `COMP-VDCM` does not compare the reported regenerative torque with an
    independent measurement of actual vehicle deceleration
    (`regen_feedback_independent_deceleration_check_enabled: false`).

- Associated Cybersecurity Goals & Damage Scenarios:
  * Protected Asset: ASSET-BRK-02 (Regenerative Torque Feedback and Friction Torque Request)
  * Security Properties: Integrity, Authenticity, Availability
  * Damage Scenario (DS-BRK-02): Insufficient combined regenerative and friction
    braking can prevent the vehicle from achieving the required deceleration.
    This can cause a collision and serious injury or death.
    Impact — Safety: Severe, Financial: Major, Operational: Severe, Privacy: Negligible.


2.3. FUNC-BRK-03: Electro-Mechanical Friction Brake Actuation
-------------------------------------------------------------
- Objective:
  Execute wheel-slip control (ABS/ESC) and drive the 48V 3-phase BLDC motors 
  inside each wheel's Smart Electro-Mechanical Brake Caliper Actuator to press 
  the brake pads against the brake disc.

- Involved Components & Interfaces:
  COMP-EBCM -> [CONN-BRK-04: Wheel-End Actuator CAN-FD Bus (2 Mbps)] -> COMP-EMBA (x4).
  The four instances share the COMP-EMBA hardware, software, interfaces, and
  security settings:
  - COMP-EMBA-FL: front-left wheel.
  - COMP-EMBA-FR: front-right wheel.
  - COMP-EMBA-RL: rear-left wheel.
  - COMP-EMBA-RR: rear-right wheel.

- Functional Behavior & Data Flow:
  1. SOME/IP Reception & ABS Loop: `COMP-EBCM` uses an NXP S32K344 with two
     Cortex-M7 cores configured as one lockstep pair. It runs FreeRTOS v10.4.1
     and `lwip` v2.1.2. The lwIP stack provides UDP/IP communication on the
     configured application port. The EBCM application processes SOME/IP service
     `0x1020`; `abs_esc_clamping_controller` (v3.4.0) computes individual wheel
     clamping forces.
  2. Actuator Command Transmission: `COMP-EBCM` transmits one command frame
     (`CAN ID 0x040`, `Target_Clamping_Force_kN`, ASSET-BRK-03) containing four
     force values in kN. The fields map to actuator instances as follows:
     - `front_left`: COMP-EMBA-FL.
     - `front_right`: COMP-EMBA-FR.
     - `rear_left`: COMP-EMBA-RL.
     - `rear_right`: COMP-EMBA-RR.
     All four actuators receive the frame on the shared CONN-BRK-04 bus.
     Each actuator uses only its assigned field.
     UDS diagnostic frames (`0x750`) also use this 2 Mbps CAN-FD sub-bus.
  3. Mechanical Pad Clamping: Each wheel's `COMP-EMBA` uses a CAN-FD-capable
     automotive MCU and an external 48V-rated three-phase BLDC motor-driver
     stage. Specific parts are not selected. Bare-Metal Firmware v2.0.0 with
     `caliper_piston_servo_driver` v2.0.0 controls the 48V BLDC ball-screw
     actuator to push the brake pads against the rotor.
  4. Normal Command Reception: Each actuator starts in normal mode with zero
     clamping demand. The following rules apply in normal mode.
     It accepts correctly formatted `0x040` frames when its assigned value is
     within zero and the configured maximum force. The last accepted value
     becomes its force target and updates local monotonic reception time.
     Invalid commands do not change the target or refresh the command timer.
     At the configured timeout, the actuator records a communication fault and
     retains its last accepted target. The next accepted command restores
     tracking and clears that fault. The servo regulates force under the active
     motor-current limit; holding a target does not command continuous maximum
     motor current.
  5. Pad-Service Priority: The EBCM routine request identifies the selected wheel
     instances. EBCM sends entry or explicit exit requests over `CONN-BRK-04`.
     On entry, each selected actuator retracts its piston to the configured
     service position. Normal `0x040` commands do not change its target and are
     not saved for later use. Completing retraction does not end service mode;
     an explicit service-exit request is required. On exit, the actuator discards
     its pre-service normal target and reception time and restores normal
     processing. It waits at the service position for a new accepted `0x040`
     command. Untargeted actuators continue their existing operation.

- Current Security & Operational Parameters (As-Built Baseline):
  * Software versions on COMP-EBCM: `freertos` v10.4.1 and `lwip` v2.1.2.
  * EBCM permits programming only while stationary, in a programming session,
    after a successful UDS `0x27` SecurityAccess unlock. Access uses the existing
    diagnostic route from VDCM; forwarding alone does not authorize programming.
    SecurityAccess uses 8-bit seed entropy (`uds_0x27_seed_entropy_bits: 8`).
    The seed/key algorithm and retry policy are not specified.
  * EBCM verifies the firmware signature against a configured trusted firmware
    signer before activation. It activates only complete, compatible images
    with valid signatures. Incomplete, unsigned, or invalidly signed images
    are rejected.
  * Bootloader rollback protection is disabled
    (`bootloader_rollback_protection: false`). An older compatible image can be
    accepted after programming authorization if its signature is valid.
  * EBCM accepts the UDS `0x31` pad-retraction routine without a SecurityAccess
    unlock and without a vehicle speed check
    (`uds_0x31_bleed_routine_speed_interlock_enabled: false`). The service-mode
    rules above define its priority and recovery. These permissions apply to
    this routine; they do not authorize other diagnostic operations.
  * `CONN-BRK-04` and `COMP-EMBA` do not verify command MACs or freshness counters 
    (`command_authentication_mac: false`, `secoc_freshness_counter_enabled: false`).
  * A manufacturer-specific UDS operation on `CONN-BRK-04` adjusts the active
    motor-current limit of the addressed actuator
    (`max_clamping_current_limiter_overrideable_via_uds: true`). It requires no
    SecurityAccess unlock or vehicle-speed check. VDCM-to-actuator diagnostic
    forwarding is not modeled.
  * Physical wiring for `CONN-BRK-04` uses unshielded twisted-pair cables
    accessible inside the exterior wheel wells without entering the cabin.
    Bus access requires physical contact with this wiring and a CAN-FD
    interface. Wireless proximity alone does not provide this access.

- Associated Cybersecurity Goals & Damage Scenarios:
  * Protected Assets:
    - ASSET-BRK-03 (Wheel Caliper Clamping Force Command - CAN ID 0x040)
      Security Properties: Integrity, Authenticity, Availability
    - ASSET-BRK-04 (EBCM & Caliper Actuator Control Firmware)
      Security Properties: Integrity, Authenticity
  * Damage Scenario (DS-BRK-03): Uneven or insufficient wheel braking can cause
    loss of directional control or increased stopping distance. This can cause
    a collision and serious injury or death.
    Impact — Safety: Severe, Financial: Major, Operational: Severe, Privacy: Negligible.
  * Damage Scenario (DS-BRK-04): Loss of anti-lock braking or damage to brake
    actuators can reduce braking performance and vehicle stability. This can
    cause a collision and serious injury or death. Damaged actuators can
    require replacement and prevent vehicle use.
    Impact — Safety: Severe, Financial: Severe, Operational: Severe, Privacy: Negligible.
